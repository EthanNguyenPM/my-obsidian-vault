# The importance of breathing

How do you run without becoming fatigued? The more you run, the better your cardiovascular system and your muscular system work in harmony to move you forward in that running motion, but a massive part of that is breathing. Breathing delivers oxygen to the muscles, so how do we breathe correctly and move that oxygen to the muscles in a more efficient manner and ultimately improve running performance? 

# Inefficient breathing effects

Correct breathing creates a balanced platform to launch performance. Not only in running but in everyday life. Now, when we get stressed in life and during high-intensity run sessions, we breathe too fast or hyperventilate; we use inefficient patterns like breathing only in the upper chest, we gulp in air through our mouth, and we blow off too much carbon dioxide. This contributes to dizziness and lightheadedness, as it squeezes the blood vessels, which diminishes oxygen flow to the muscles and the brain

Now we can change all that; Breathing can be trained and enhanced, but like all training, we have to start within capacity and then gradually work up to improve our breathing performance. We need to breathe in through our nose as much as possible

# Techniques for nasal breathing

You need to breathe in through your nose as much as possible when you run. This helps humidify the air, filter the air, and also helps engage the diaphragm more; therefore, your breathing is going to be more efficient. We have to breathe a bit more slowly, a little bit deeper, and definitely using that nose. 

It has been shown that if you breathe in through your nose, you actually release nitric oxide into the system via the sinuses, which further improves oxygen uptake by up to 18 percent; so let's go over three practical breathing patterns that we can use at different intensities of our run to help us be more mindful of our breathing. We're just going to improve efficiency and ultimately performance 

There are ones that you can use practically when you're running; it's really important, though, to realize that your posture and your ability to breathe correctly are linked, so before you start thinking about the breathing, make sure your posture is upright, shoulders down, head immediately on top of the spine, so that upright posture is going to allow the correct breathing.

## Slow in and slow out

So the first breathing pattern is simply to slow in and slow out, so this can be used on your slow recovery runs or even your long distance. You don't have to do this technique all the time, but it's good to check in and just see how you are and how balanced your breathing is. So with this, you breathe in for four or six steps and then breathe out for four or six steps.

- Breathe in: 4-6 steps 
- Breathe out: 4-6 steps 
## Fast in and slow out

In this technique, breathe quite quickly through your nose for maybe two steps and exhale over around four steps. So you want to use this breathing pattern for your more up-tempo workout, at a pace between 10k and your half marathon race pace.

- Breathe in: 2 steps
- Breathe out: 4-6 steps

## Fast in and fast out

You want to save this for the higher-intensity, short, sharp reps.

- Breathe in: 2 steps
- Breathe out: 2 steps
