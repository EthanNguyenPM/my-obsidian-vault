_An increasing number of fathers are becoming their children's primary caregivers while mothers work outside the home. This trend is mainly explained by changing career opportunities and attitudes towards gender roles. I consider it a largely positive development, as it allows families to organize their lives according to their needs.

_One major reason for this trend is that women now have greater access to education and professional opportunities. In some households, the mother earns more and has better opportunities for promotion than the father. It may therefore make financial sense for her to remain employed while he cares for their children. Changing social attitudes also plays a role. As parenting is no longer seen solely as a woman's responsibility, more fathers may feel comfortable taking on responsibilities that were traditionally expected of women.

_In my view, this trend benefits families and society alike. Allowing parents to divide paid work and childcare can help them protect household income while ensuring that children receive consistent care. Fathers who spend time caring for their children have more opportunities to develop closer relationships with them. Meanwhile, mothers could continue pursuing careers rather than being expected to leave their jobs simply because they are women. Children may grow up with a more flexible understanding of family responsibilities, recognizing that both men and women can be capable caregivers and breadwinners.

_Admittedly, a parent who leaves employment may face difficulties returning to work later. This challenge can affect either parent and does not make stay-at-home fatherhood inherently negative. Flexible working arrangements and support for returning employees could reduce this disadvantage.

_In conclusion, this trend reflects both economic considerations and changing gender expectations. I believe it is positive overall, provided that both parents can agree on how to share responsibility voluntarily and the arrangement meets their family's needs
