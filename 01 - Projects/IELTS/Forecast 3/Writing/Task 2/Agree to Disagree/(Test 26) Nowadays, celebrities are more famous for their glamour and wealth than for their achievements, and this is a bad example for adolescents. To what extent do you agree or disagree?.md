In the present day, celebrities often attract public attention through their extravagant wealth and glamorous lifestyles rather than their genuine accomplishments. Some people argue that this trend sets a harmful example for young people, and I completely agree with this viewpoint.

Firstly, the constant display of material wealth by famous figures encourages adolescents to adopt a **materialistic mindset**. Thanks to the proliferation of social media, teenagers can encounter images of luxury cars, designer clothes, and opulent mansions in a split second on their smartphones. Because these glamorous snapshots are far more visible than the years of hard work behind them, impressionable young people often equate success solely with **material possessions**. Consequently, adolescents who seek validation from their peers may feel pressured to purchase expensive items just to **project an image of wealth**, leading to unnecessary financial strain on themselves and their families.

Furthermore, glorifying effortless glamour can **undermine the value of hard work** and perseverance among the youth. When young people repeatedly see influencers gaining fame and fortune simply through their physical appearance or extravagant lifestyles, they may lose the motivation to pursue education or develop practical skills. Instead of striving for long-term career achievements, many teenagers fall into the trap of seeking **instant gratification** or shortcuts to fame. Over time, this distorted perception not only stunts their personal growth but also creates a generation that prioritizes **superficial status** over meaningful contributions to society.

In conclusion, I firmly believe that celebrating celebrities for their wealth and glamour rather than their achievements negatively impacts adolescents. This trend not only fuels unhealthy materialism among young people but also discourages them from working hard to achieve genuine success.
