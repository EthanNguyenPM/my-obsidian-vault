Stacked charts compare the proportion of households with one to six members in the UK in 1981 and 2001.

Overall, two-person households accounted for the largest percentage in both years, while six-person households represented the smallest share. The proportions of one- or two-person households increased, whereas those of larger households declined. 

In 1981, households with two occupants were the most common, making up 31%, while single households recorded 17%. By 2001, these figures had risen to 34% and 26%, respectively. The former households showed the largest increase, at 9%, overtaking two- and four-person households to become the second-largest category.

By contrast, the percentage of households containing three or four people fell by three percentage points, from 20% to 17% and 18% to 15%. The share of five-person households also declined, dropping from 8% to 6%. Finally, six-person households remained the least common category, with their proportion decreasing from 6% in 1981 to just 2% in 2001.
