The bar charts show how the figures for men and women getting married varied across six age groups in a particular country in 1996 and 2008. 

Overall, the figure for women peaked at ages 25-29 in both years. Among the men, both the 25-29 and 30-34 age groups shared first place in 1997, while the 35-39-year-old group led in 2008. Female figures were higher below age 30, with the reverse pattern across the three oldest groups.

Among women, those aged 25–29 recorded the highest figure in both years, at around 12. The 30–34 age group ranked second, with its figure rising from 7 in 1996 to 8 in 2008. Over the same period, the figure for women aged 20–24 halved from 6 to 3, while that for the 35–39 group fell from 4 to just over 2. The 40–44 age group remained steady at 2, and the lowest figure was recorded among 16–19-year-olds, falling from about 1 to just under 0.5.

With regard to men, the figures for men aged 25-29 and 30-34 both stood at 9 in 1996. By 2008, the former had slipped to 8, while the latter had climbed to 11. The 35-39 group saw the largest increase, from about 7 to just over 11, putting it slightly ahead of the 30-34 group. Meanwhile, the figure for men aged 40-44 declined from 5.5 to 3.5, and the 20-24 group edged down to 2. Neither chart shows a figure for men aged 16-19.
