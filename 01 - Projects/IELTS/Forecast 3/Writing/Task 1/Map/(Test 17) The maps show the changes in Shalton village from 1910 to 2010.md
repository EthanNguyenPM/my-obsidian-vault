_The maps illustrate the transformation of Shalton village over a 100-year period from 1910 to 2010.

_Overall, the village witnessed a significant residential expansion, characterized by the proliferation of houses at the expense of green space and a large house. Commercial facilities also changed, while a school, a post office, and main road networks remained in their original positions.

_In 1910, a large house surrounded by trees occupied the triangular heart of the village, bounded by School Road to the west, London Road to the east, and High Street to the south. By 2010, this green space had been cleared to make way for a cluster of houses, served by a newly constructed side road branching off London Road. A grocer's on the west side of the triangular base was superseded by a cafe, while a baker's shop was replaced by a residential building. Housing remained flanked by these two sites.

_Looking at the periphery, a butcher's shop on the western side of School Road was transformed into a supermarket with an adjoining parking lot. To its north, a bank was added on the site of a former house. Additionally, a row of residential properties emerged along the eastern stretch of London Road, while the number of houses south of High Street increased from two to four. Meanwhile, the school and the post office were retained.

The maps compare the layout of Shalton village in 1910 and 2010.

Overall, the village became considerably more residential over the century, with housing replacing a large house and surrounding trees. Commercial facilities also changed, while the school, post office and main road network remained in their original locations.

In 1910, School Road, London Road and High Street enclosed a triangular central area containing a large house surrounded by trees. By 2010, the house and trees had been removed to make way for housing around a new access road leading off London Road. On the northern side of High Street, the grocer’s was replaced by a café, while housing occupied the former baker’s site. The residential site between these premises remained in use.

Outside the central area, the butcher’s on the western side of School Road was replaced by a supermarket with an adjoining parking area. To its north, a bank occupied the site of a former house. A row of houses was constructed along the eastern side of London Road, while the number of houses south of High Street increased from two to four. The school and post office were retained.
