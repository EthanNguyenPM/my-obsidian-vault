The table illustrates the proportion of expenditure allocated to healthcare—broken down into public and private sectors—across five countries in 2002.

Overall, France recorded the highest figures across all three categories, whereas Japan spent the lowest proportion of its budget on healthcare. In addition, public spending accounted for the vast majority of total health expenditure in every nation surveyed.

Looking first at total and public healthcare expenditure, France led the table with an overall figure of 11.4%, comprising 8.6% from public funds. Germany followed in second place, dedicating 9.3% in total and 7% to the public sector, while the USA ranked third at 8.6% and 6%, respectively. By contrast, Italy and Japan recorded notably lower overall shares, at 7.7% and 7.2%. Their public spending figures were similarly modest, standing at 5.3% for Italy and 5.8% for Japan.

Turning to private healthcare spending, this category represented a much smaller fraction in all five nations, remaining below 3% across the board. France again topped the list at 2.8%, closely followed by the USA at 2.6%. Italy and Germany exhibited comparable figures, at 2.4% and 2.3% respectively, whereas Japan had the lowest private expenditure at just 1.4%—exactly half of France’s figure.
