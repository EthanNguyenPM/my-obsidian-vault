The table illustrates the number of immigrants residing in each other's countries in 2011 across five European countries.

Overall, Germany and Britain hosted the largest populations from the other countries, while Poland **accommodated** by far the fewest. Poles formed the largest group living elsewhere within the five countries, while Spanish nationals constitute the smallest.

Regarding the three popular destinations, Germany **hosted** 1,198,000 residents from other countries, including 556,000 Italians and 426,000 Poles. Meanwhile, Britain ranked second, with 1,037,000 residents, of whom 550,000 were Polish, and 297,000 were German. Spain followed with 391,000 Britons, and Britons accounted for its largest group, at 391,000. Germans and Italians living there numbered 196,000 and 188,000, respectively.

With regard to the remaining countries, the corresponding totals for Italy and Poland were considerably lower, at 196,000 and 6,000 immigrants. Polish people made up the largest group in Italy, numbering 106,000 residents, followed by 42,000 Germans, 29,000 Britons, and 19,000 Spaniards. In Poland, Germans predominated, at 4,400, while British, Italian, and Spanish people were much smaller, at 760, 670, and 170.
