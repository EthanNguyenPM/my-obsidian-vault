The allocation of public funds to space exploration versus addressing global problem has been a subject of considerable debate. While some argue that space ventures is an unjustifiable expense, concomitant with urgent unresolved, others highlight the potential long-term benefits of space exploration. I believe the governments should strike a balance between combating these pressing needs and funding the space exploration to help build a more sustainable future.

On the one hand, the investment in the exploration of outer space has historically driven technological innovation and improved humanity's long-term prospects. Most importantly, many of the Earth's essential resources, such as natural gas and crude oil, are finite while demand for them continue to rise alongside economic and population growth. Despite the widespread adoption of renewable energy sources, such as solar and wind power, their supply remains limited and intermittent, depending on weather conditions. Concurrently, the population explosion is also exerting mounting pressure on the natural environment, contributing to deforestation, anthropogenic climate change, and habitat loss. In the event of persistent compounding problems without effective policy, posterity may have to contend with severe pollution, acute resource shortage, and deplorable living conditions. Some opponent even contend that it is premature to invest in the space exploration, given that the need for such problems may not emerge in the short term. However, initiating research at an early stage could be instrumental in preparing humanity for an unpredictable future. 

On the other hand, there are dire global concerns occurring around the world that government bodies should not overlook. Millions of people continue to suffer from atrocious poverty, inadequate access to education and healthcare system, and escalating consequences of human-induced climate change, all of which require immediate interventions and substantial financial resources. Redirecting resources to space exploration could yield tangible improvements in the quality of life for vulnerable population. The vast sums allocated to hospitals and disaster relief efforts help save countless lives in a short timeframe. Nevertheless, abandoning space study in favor of short-term measures would be short-sighted, since numerous technological breakthroughs originating from space research, including satellite-based weather forecasting and global communication networks, has proven crucial in tackling precisely these humanitarian and environmental challenges. Consequently, rather than treating two priorities as mutually exclusive, governments should adopt a balanced funding strategy that ameliorates pressing crises while sustaining incremental investment in space research.

In conclusion, although overcoming immediate global obstacles such as poverty and climate change warrant immediate government fundings and significant sources, dismissing space study could forfeit the valuable long-term benefits for humanity. Governments should allocate public funds judiciously, ensuring that emergent crises are managed effectively without foreclosing scientific progress that space mission promises for future generations.
