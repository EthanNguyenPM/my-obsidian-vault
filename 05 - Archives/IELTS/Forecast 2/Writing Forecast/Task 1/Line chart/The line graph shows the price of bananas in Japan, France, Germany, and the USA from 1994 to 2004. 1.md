The line chart compares the banana prices across 4 countries, namely Japan, France, Germany, and the USA, from 1994 to 2004, measured in US dollars per kilogram.

Overall, Japan was generally the most expensive country for bananas, while the USA had relatively stable prices and remained the cheapest among the countries over the period shown. Furthermore, France and Germany experienced moderate variations, both displaying broadly similar trends.

In 1994, Japan's banana prices started at approximately 1.9 dollars per kilogram, and then rose steeply to a peak of about $2.9 in the following year. Nevertheless, its figure dropped to $2.0 in 1997, and subsequently fluctuated between $1.9 and $2.2 from 1997 to 2001. Over the next three years, the cost of bananas in Japan recovered strongly, ending the period at about $2.5, remaining the highest overall. In contrast, the USA maintained very steady prices, increasing slightly from around $0.9 in 1994 to $1.1 in 1996, before staying close to this level for the rest of the period

Turning to France and Germany, France's banana prices were relatively constant at roughly $1.7 from 1994 to 1997, whereas Germany's fluctuated only slightly, moving between about $1.3 to $1.5. Both figures shared the same pattern of fluctuation in the following years. Notably, in 2001, the French price reached approximately $1.7, briefly overtaking Japan. However, it then fell sharply to around $1.0 in 2003, becoming the cheapest among the four countries, before returning to second place behind Japan at $1.6. Meanwhile, Germany's banana prices declined gradually after 1999 and remained at around $1.2 toward the end of the period.
