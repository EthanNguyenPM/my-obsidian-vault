The pie charts illustrate the proportion of energy production from six distinct sources in a particular country over a span of 18 years, from 1985 to 2003. 

Overall, oil remained the dominant energy source in both years, despite a significant decline. Nuclear also fell sharply, while natural gas, coal, and other renewables increased. In contrast, hydro remained unchanged at a low level. 

In 1985, oil accounted for more than half of the country's total energy production, at precisely 52 percent. This was followed by nuclear and natural gas, which constituted 22 percent and 13 percent, respectively. Meanwhile, coal made up a relatively modest proportion of 8 percent, doubling the share of hydro at 4 percent. Other renewables were the least significant source, at just 1%.

By 2003, the energy production profile had undergone significant changes. The percentage of oil had dropped substantially to 39%, although it was still the largest contributor. Nuclear energy saw an even sharper relative decline, falling to 8%. Conversely, natural gas grew by 10 percent, becoming the second largest source, while coal followed closely at 22%, just 1 percent lower than natural gas.
