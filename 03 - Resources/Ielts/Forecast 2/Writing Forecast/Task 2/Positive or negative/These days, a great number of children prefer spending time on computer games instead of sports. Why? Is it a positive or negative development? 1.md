In recent years, an increasing number of children have shown a preference for playing computer games rather than participating in sports. This phenomenon can be attributed to several factors, particularly inadequate parental supervision and the highly engaging nature of modern games. In my opinion, this is largely a negative development because of its detrimental effects on children's physical and social development.

One major reason for this trend is that many parents are unable to closely monitor how their children spend their leisure time. In numerous households, both parents work full-time and therefore have limited opportunities to encourage outdoor activities or participate in sports with their children. As a result, youngsters are often left to entertain themselves, and computer games become the most convenient option. Another contributing factor is the increasingly immersive design of modern video games. Game developers invest enormous resources in creating realistic graphics, compelling storylines, and sophisticated reward systems that continuously stimulate players. Consequently, children can easily become absorbed in virtual environments that appear far more exciting than traditional sporting activities.

I believe this development is predominantly negative. The most obvious drawback is the deterioration of children's physical health. Spending prolonged periods in front of screens promotes a sedentary lifestyle, which may lead to obesity, poor cardiovascular health, and vision-related problems. By contrast, regular participation in sports improves physical fitness and encourages healthy habits from an early age. Furthermore, an excessive reliance on computer games may hinder children's social development. Sporting activities require participants to communicate, cooperate, and resolve conflicts with teammates, whereas gaming is often a more isolated experience. Consequently, children who rarely engage in sports may struggle to develop essential interpersonal skills that are crucial for their future personal and professional lives.

In conclusion, insufficient parental supervision and the captivating nature of contemporary video games are the primary reasons why many children choose gaming over sports. Although computer games may provide entertainment, I believe that this trend is largely negative because it undermines both the physical well-being and social development of young people.
