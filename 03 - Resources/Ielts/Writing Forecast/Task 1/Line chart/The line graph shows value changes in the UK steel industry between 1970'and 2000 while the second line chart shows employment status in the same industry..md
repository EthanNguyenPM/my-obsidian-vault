The first line chart illustrates the changes in total demand, production, and imports in the UK steel industry from 1970 to 2000, while the second chart shows the number of people employed in the same sector over this period.

Overall, both total demand and production declined considerably throughout the period, whereas steel imports rose steadily. Employment also declined significantly, indicating a major contraction in the steel industry.

At the beginning of the period, the steel domestic production stood at approximately 165,000 tonnes. This figure was insufficient to meet the demand, which was around 200,000 tonnes. Steel imports were relatively negligible. Over the following three decades, both total demand and domestic production plummeted markedly, falling to around 100,000 and 80,000, respectively, in 2000. In contrast, steel imports increased steadily, reaching 75,000 tonnes at the end of the period, a figure slightly lower than the UK's steel production.

Turning to employment, the industry initially employed about 50,000 workers in 1970. This number then declined in parallel with the reduction in the total demand. A particularly sharp drop was observed between 1980 and 1990, when workers fell steeply from nearly 40,000 to just over 20,000. By 2000, the employment had reached a low of 17,000 workers, reflecting a major contraction of the industry.
