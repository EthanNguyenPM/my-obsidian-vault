## Part 1

### Do you like cooking?

Actually, I’m not really a big fan of cooking. I find it quite **time-consuming** and to be frank, I’m not very good at it yet. That’s why I tend to **opt for quick and easy dishes** or sometimes **order take-away**. But I do think it’s an important **life skill**, so I’m trying to **pick it up gradually** by watching cooking tutorials and **trying my hand at** some basic recipes.

### Do you cook very often?

To be honest, I **don’t cook very often** at all. I find it pretty **time-consuming**, and I’m not particularly talented in the kitchen yet. Most days I just **opt for convenience food** or **get food delivered** because it’s faster and more practical for my lifestyle. Having said that, I know cooking is an important life skill, so I’m slowly trying to pick it up by watching online tutorials

### What dishes can you cook well? / What are you good at cooking?

Hmm, I wouldn’t say I’m **exceptionally good**, but I can cook a few things **reasonably well**. My best dish is probably **gỏi cuốn** – the fresh spring rolls – because they’re quite easy once you get the hang of rolling them tightly, and they’re always a hit with friends.

I’m also okay at making basic stir-fries and soups, but I still need more practice for complicated dishes like phở!

### Who taught you how to cook? / Did you learn to cook when you were a child?

"To be honest, I didn’t **learn to cook properly** when I was a child – I was more interested in playing outside! My mum handled most of the cooking back then.

But she did **teach me a few simple things** as I grew up, like how to make **trứng chiên** (fried eggs) or basic stir-fries. I only really started getting serious about it in my late teens, mostly by watching her and **picking up tips** along the way.

Now I enjoy it more, especially dishes like **gỏi cuốn** that remind me of family time

So, yes – family is definitely my main teacher!

### Do you prefer home-cooked food or eating out?

Well, it really depends on the situation, but on the whole, I tend to prefer home-cooked food. I find it more satisfying and cost-effective, plus it’s usually healthier since I can avoid too much oil or MSG

### Who usually does the cooking in your home/family?

"In my family, **my mum** is the one who **usually does the cooking**. She's been **in charge of the kitchen** for as long as I can remember, and she prepares almost all our **daily home-cooked meals**.

On special occasions or weekends, my **grandmother** sometimes **takes over** and makes her signature dishes like **phở** or **bún bò Huế** – she’s the real expert when it comes to **getting the flavours just right**!

These days, I help out more, especially with simple tasks, but **mum** is definitely the **main cook** in our household!"
### Do you think cooking is difficult? / Do you want to learn how to cook?

"Yes, definitely – I **really want to learn how to cook** better! I already know some basics, but there are still so many Vietnamese dishes I’d love to **master**, like **perfecting the broth** for **phở** or making **bún chả cá** the Nha Trang way.

I think cooking is a **valuable life skill**, and it’s actually quite **rewarding** when you see your family enjoy what you’ve made.

I usually watch YouTube tutorials or help my mum in the kitchen to **pick up new techniques** – it’s fun and relaxing!"

## Part 3

### Do you think men can be good cooks?

Yes, definitely – I think **men can be excellent cooks**, just as good as women, if not better in some cases! Cooking is a **life skill** that has nothing to do with gender; it’s all about **practice**, **passion**, and **attention to detail**.

In Vietnam, many street food stalls are run by men, and they often **master the art of** dishes like **phở**, **bún chả cá**, or grilled seafood – the flavours are always spot on!

In my opinion, **stereotypes** about cooking being ‘women’s work’ are outdated – anyone can **whip up a delicious meal** if they put their heart into it!"

### In your country, do most people enjoy cooking?

Well, it really depends on the generation and where people live, but overall, **many Vietnamese people do enjoy cooking** – it’s considered an important **life skill** and a way to **preserve family traditions**. In most families, especially in rural areas or smaller cities like Nha Trang, people **take pleasure in** going to the market for **fresh local ingredients** and **whipping up** delicious meals like **gỏi cuốn** or grilled seafood.

However, in big cities, with longer working hours and the rise of street food culture, **eating out** has become more popular – surveys show people dine out 3-4 times a week more often now.

So, while **home cooking** is still cherished, especially for **quality family time**, **eating out** is a growing trend!"
