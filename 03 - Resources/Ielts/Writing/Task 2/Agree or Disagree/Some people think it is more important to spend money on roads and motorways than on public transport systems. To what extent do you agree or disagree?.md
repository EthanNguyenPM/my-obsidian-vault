There is an ongoing debate over whether authorities should invest funds in roads and motorways or the public transport system. Spending on roads and motorways is undoubtedly important, while I believe public transit deserves greater attention due to its long-term environmental and social benefits.

On the one hand, allocating more funds to roads and motorways can bring substantial merits, particularly in developing countries or rural areas. In some regions, many roads are poorly maintained and unsafe, making transportation both inefficient and treacherous. Improving the road conditions would facilitate the movement of goods and people, boosting the economic activities and reducing the risk of traffic accidents. For example, better road networks allow trucks to transport essential goods more efficiently, which supports trade and ensures timely delivery. Furthermore, expanding and upgrading road systems can help alleviate congestion in urban areas, foster overall mobility, and quality of life

On the other hand, investment in public transport offers more sustainable benefits, especially in densely populated cities. One of the most pressing issues is air pollution, which is largely caused by the excessive use of private vehicles. In contrast, public transit, such as buses, trams, trains, and metros, can carry large numbers of passengers at once, making them far more environmentally friendly. Encouraging people to shift from personal vehicles to public ones can significantly cut down on carbon emissions and alleviate the chances of gridlock during peak hours. Moreover, such a public mode of transport is often more affordable and accessible, which helps enhance social equality and urban living standards.
