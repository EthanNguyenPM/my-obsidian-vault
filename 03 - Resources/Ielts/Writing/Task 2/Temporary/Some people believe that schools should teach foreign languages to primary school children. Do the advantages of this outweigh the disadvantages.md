There are several compelling reasons why the inclusion of foreign languages in the primary school curriculum is highly conducive to general academic development. It is widely acknowledged that children at this developmental stage are naturally characterized by <mark class="hltr-red">heightened neuroplasticity</mark>, thereby enabling them to acquire and retain knowledge more efficiently than individuals at later stages of life. Since mastering an additional language is extremely demanding, requiring learners to adapt to unfamiliar grammatical structures and pronunciation rules, it demands both cognitive capacity and sustained exposure. As a result, elementary school students <mark class="hltr-red">are exceptionally receptive to</mark> second-language acquisition, allowing them to attain higher levels of proficiency and lay a solid foundation for long-term academic and professional success.
