In the past, when our agriculture was still far more optimal, food ingredients were not extensively calorie-dense. This situation prompted people's diet to consist of larger proportions of vegetables, thereby in order to promoting satiety more quickly. Nevertheless, with the advancement of agricultural practices, many rudimentary tools have been replaced by modern mechanized equipment, significantly enhancing productivity and efficiency. As a result, food can now be mass-produced at a substantially lower cost and in greater quantities. This paradigm shift has led high-energy foods to become more accessible, leading to a transformation in dietary habits and contributing to chronic health issues such as obesity and nutritional imbalances if left unchecked.
