Due to its unique characteristics, social media is gradually replacing conventional forms of face-to-face interaction. After considering both sides of this tendency, I believe the benefits brought about by the surging prevalence of social platforms substantially outweigh the potential drawbacks, as the advantages operate on a <mark class="hltr-red">societal scale</mark> while the disadvantages remain largely manageable <mark class="hltr-red">at an individual level</mark>.

The social network offers significant benefits owing to its cost-effective and <mark class="hltr-red">multimodal nature</mark>, particularly when compared with traditional in-person interaction. While arranging a physical meeting demands travel expenses and time commitment, communicating with others via social media is generally inexpensive, enabling users and businesses to transcend geographical barriers. Furthermore, such platforms allow the dissemination of news and spontaneous updates to many people at the same time, boosting the speed and reach of communication far beyond what traditional interpersonal interaction can achieve. These merits, which apply to billions of users globally, represent a <mark class="hltr-red">structural transformation</mark> in how societies exchange information, and this impact is too <mark class="hltr-red">significant to be offset</mark> by interpersonal risks.

While social media may <mark class="hltr-red">give rise to</mark> issues that users cannot overlook, these concerns <mark class="hltr-red">are comparatively limited</mark> in scope. Although virtual platforms <mark class="hltr-red">facilitate</mark> online interactions and generate social capital, people's attention to each relationship becomes increasingly divided, making social connections more superficial and less intimate. This is problematic because, as social beings, humans have a propensity to seek meaningful conversations and close emotional bonds with others for genuine emotional exchange. Consequently, increased superficiality and reduced intimacy can affect online users' mental well-being and potentially damage close-knit relationships. However, these are challenges that can be addressed through <mark class="hltr-red">individual choices</mark> about how one manages their <mark class="hltr-red">online presence</mark>, rather than <mark class="hltr-red">inherent flaws</mark> of the technology itself.

In conclusion, whereas the excessive reliance on social networking sites risks <mark class="hltr-red">eroding the depth of</mark> personal relationships and impacting mental well-being, these drawbacks are outweighed by <mark class="hltr-red">the transformative capacity</mark> of social media to connect people across distances and <mark class="hltr-red">democratize communication</mark>. The former represents manageable personal challenges, the latter <mark class="hltr-red">constitutes a far-reaching societal benefit</mark> that redefines the boundaries of human interaction.
