This phenomenon is attributed to rapid urbanization, which greatly exerts pressure on public infrastructures and contributes to social issues like unemployment and destitution. Nevertheless, this problem can be mitigated through the investment in communal facilities and stronger social support systems.

Secondly, administrators can provide comprehensive support to the underprivileged group of people, enhancing individuals' skills and employability, which allows them to secure stable jobs. Through allocating expenses on refurbishment and subsidizing for disadvantaged occupants, governments can diminish the presence of such concerns.

In many countries, particularly developing ones, the quality of life in large cities has been declining in recent years. This trend is largely driven by rapid urbanisation, which places considerable strain on infrastructure and exacerbates social problems such as unemployment and crime. Nevertheless, these issues can be addressed through strategic investment and targeted social policies.

One major cause of the deterioration in urban living standards is the continuous migration from rural areas to cities. In search of better employment and educational opportunities, large numbers of people relocate to metropolitan areas, often at a pace that exceeds the capacity of existing infrastructure. Consequently, essential services such as public transport, healthcare, and housing become overcrowded and inefficient. For instance, in many megacities, commuters are forced to endure long travel times due to inadequate transport systems, while affordable housing remains scarce. In addition, the labour market is frequently unable to absorb the growing workforce, resulting in high levels of unemployment. This, in turn, may push some individuals towards illegal activities, further undermining the safety and overall quality of urban life.

To tackle these challenges, governments need to adopt a combination of infrastructural and social measures. First and foremost, greater investment in urban infrastructure is crucial. Expanding public transportation networks, improving healthcare facilities, and constructing affordable housing can significantly enhance living conditions and reduce congestion. Equally important is the implementation of effective social support programmes. Providing vocational training and financial assistance can equip disadvantaged individuals with practical skills, thereby increasing their employability. For example, government-funded training schemes can help unemployed workers transition into high-demand sectors. By reducing joblessness and poverty, such initiatives can also contribute to lower crime rates and a more stable urban environment.

In conclusion, the declining quality of life in major cities is primarily the result of rapid population growth and insufficient infrastructure. However, with well-planned investments and comprehensive social policies, these problems can be effectively mitigated.
