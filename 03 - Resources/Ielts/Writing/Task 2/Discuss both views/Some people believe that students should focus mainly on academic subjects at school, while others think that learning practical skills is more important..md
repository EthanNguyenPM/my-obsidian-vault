Some people believe that academic subjects should be fully focused on during the time at school, while opponents argue that students are incentivized to redirect their efforts and energy to practical skills instead. Although both viewpoints are defensible, I believe we should balance these two in their formative years.

From one perspective, academic subjects, such as mathematics, science, or languages, are a crucial element for landing stable and well-paying jobs and helping them enter higher education. This is widely acknowledged as many demanding work requires students to have a strong background and foundation in these subjects, thereby allocating an extensive attention span to them plays an important role in shaping the future of a child, particularly for those who are seniors in high school and ready to enter university. Furthermore, in a modern world, where there is pervasive misinformation, it is a critical thinking skill that pupils can be equipped with while acquiring these subjects, enabling them to discern the truth and to raise their voices if necessary. 

On the other hand, practical skills are increasingly demonstrating the important competency that should not be ignored. With the proliferation of a fast-paced society, a number of job demands from employees to be more adaptable with some soft skills, namely communication, teamwork, management, and resilience under high pressure. Mastering these skills not only helps individuals be employed but also gains more opportunities for salary and offers, especially when individuals learn practical skills from an early stage. Furthermore, not every student is pursuing tertiary or high-school education; the adoption of vocational skills enables them to have a livelihood. In Germany, for instance, dual education that combines vocational and academic knowledge is applied.

In conclusion, while enhancing academic skills is undoubtedly essential, students should not overlook the significance of practical competencies. I believe schools should incorporate both skills in the curriculum that help students be ready for their future.
