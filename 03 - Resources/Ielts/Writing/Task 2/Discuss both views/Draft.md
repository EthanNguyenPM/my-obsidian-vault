The contemporary landscape of ongoing debate over whether the study approach would be optimal among students in schools. Several people argue that studying in a group will entail significant benefits, while opponents contend that studying on one's own volition plays a more crucial role. Despite the validity of both perspectives, I contend that it would be more effective if students alternate between self-study and group study, as each method offers distinctive benefits.

On the one hand, one major advantage of studying in a group is that it makes learning more engaging and interactive. By doing so, students are required to explain ideas, ask clarifying questions, and discuss their peers' errors, thereby enabling them to identify the gaps in their knowledge and adjust the learning plan to be more effective. Furthermore, group study is also a great way to enhance social skills, helping students step out of an isolation zone, strengthen communication competencies, and build strong connections with their peers. 

On the other hand, self-study serves as an essential factor in shaping the development of a learner. First and foremost, studying independently allows an individual to move at their own pace, as people in the group hardly wait for each other, and a superior member makes the most of thinking, while the weaker one struggles to keep up. However, an independent student needs to build discipline and concentration.

In conclusion, while both dimensions of studying have their own merits, the combination of the two approaches can reap a great results.
