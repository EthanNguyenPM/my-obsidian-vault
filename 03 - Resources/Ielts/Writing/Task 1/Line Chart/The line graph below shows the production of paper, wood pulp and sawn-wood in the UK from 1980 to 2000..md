The line graph illustrates changes in the production of three forest industry products—paper and packaging, wood pulp, and sawn wood in the UK over a 20-year period from 1980 to 2000. The figures are given in million tonnes.

Overall, paper and packaging production experienced significant growth and remained the dominant category throughout the period. In contrast, sawn-wood output declined steadily, while wood pulp showed only minor fluctuations.

In 1980, paper and packaging production stood at approximately 230 million tonnes, the highest figure among the three. It then rose gradually to around 250 million tonnes by 1990, before surging dramatically to 350 million tonnes in 2000, marking the most substantial increase overall.

By comparison, sawn wood began as the second-largest category at 200 million tonnes in 1980. However, its production fell consistently, reaching about 150 million tonnes in 1990 and dropping further to roughly 130 million tonnes by the end of the period, making it the lowest by 2000.

Meanwhile, wood pulp production started at around 160 million tonnes in 1980. It decreased slightly to 150 million tonnes in 1990 (matching sawn-wood at that point), before recovering marginally to 160 million tonnes in 2000, remaining relatively stable compared to the other two products.
