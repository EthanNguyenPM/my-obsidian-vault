The line graph illustrates the amount of air pollutants emitted in the UK from four different sources, namely transport, industry, households, and total emissions, between 1990 and 2005.

Overall, the total volume of air pollution experienced a significant decline throughout the period. Industry was consistently the largest contributor to air pollutants despite a substantial reduction, while household emissions fell steadily to become the smallest source by 2005. In contrast, pollution from transport remained relatively stable.

In 1990, total air pollutants stood at approximately 7 million tonnes before decreasing gradually to 6 million in 1993 and 5 million in 1996. This downward trend continued, reaching around 4 million tonnes in 1999 and 2002, before falling further to roughly 3 million tonnes by the end of the period.

Industrial emissions were the primary source of pollution at the beginning of the period, at about 5.5 million tonnes. However, this figure dropped dramatically to just under 3 million tonnes in 1996 and continued to decline steadily to around 2 million tonnes in 2005. Household pollution also decreased considerably, from roughly 1 million tonnes in 1990 to only 0.2 million tonnes by 2005.

By contrast, transport emissions fluctuated only slightly over the 15-year period. Starting at around 1 million tonnes in 1990, the figure rose marginally to just above 1 million tonnes in 1996 and then remained relatively unchanged until 2005.
