The line graph illustrates the percentage of people in four different age groups who visited the cinema at least once a month between 1984 and 2003.

Overall, cinema attendance increased in all age groups over the period. People aged 15–24 consistently recorded the highest proportion of cinema-goers, whereas those aged 35 and over remained the least frequent visitors. In addition, younger age groups experienced more significant growth compared to older people.

In 1984, approximately 15% of individuals aged 15–24 went to the cinema monthly, compared to around 10% of those aged 7–14. The figure for the 15–24 age group rose steadily throughout the period despite some fluctuations, reaching a peak of nearly 58% in 2002 before ending slightly lower in 2003.

Similarly, the percentage of cinema visitors aged 7–14 increased overall from roughly 10% to 30%. Although the figure fluctuated during the late 1990s, it climbed sharply after 2000 and became the second-highest category by the end of the period.

Turning to older age groups, the proportion of people aged 25–35 also showed a considerable rise, increasing from around 5% in 1984 to approximately 30% in 2003. The most noticeable growth occurred after the mid-1990s.

By contrast, people aged 35 and over had the lowest cinema attendance throughout the period. Starting at only about 1% in 1984, the figure increased gradually and reached just over 10% by 2003.
