The line chart compares the changes in worldwide populations for four types of fish, namely Atlantic Cod, Haddock, Yellowfin Tuna, and Swordfish, over a span of 25 years starting in 1982.

Overall, Atlantic Cod and Yellowfin Tuna experienced noticeable fluctuations, followed by an overall decline, whereas Haddock remained at a negligible level with a slight upward trend. By contrast, Swordfish consistently accounted for the smallest population throughout the entire period.

In 1982, Atlantic Cod was by far the most common species, with approximately 230,000. However, its figure fell sharply to 150,000 in 1992. Although its population recovered moderately to nearly 170,000, it subsequently declined again, ending the period at about 140,000 in 2007.

A different pattern was seen for Yellowfin Tuna. Starting at roughly 150,000 in 1982, its number rose considerably and peaked at about 200,000 in the next 10 years. Nevertheless, this upward trend was not sustained, as the figure dropped steadily thereafter to approximately 130,000 in 2002 before reaching only 100,000 by the end of the period. 

Meanwhile, haddock showed a minimal variation over the 20-year span. Its population remained close to 70,000 between 1982 and 1992, before edging up gradually to just over 70,000 in 2002. Swordfish, on the other hand, recorded the lowest numbers throughout, staying virtually unchanged at roughly 70,000 fish worldwide.
