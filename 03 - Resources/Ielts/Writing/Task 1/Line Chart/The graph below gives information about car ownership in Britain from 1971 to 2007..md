The line graph illustrates changes in car ownership among households in the UK between 1971 and 2007.

Overall, the percentage of households without a car declined considerably over the period, whereas ownership of two or more cars increased steadily. By contrast, the proportion of households owning one car remained relatively stable and consistently represented the largest category.

In 1971, almost half of UK households did not own a car, at approximately 48%. This figure fell gradually to around 40% by 1979 before continuing to decline to about 25% in 2003. It then rose slightly to roughly 26% in the final year. Meanwhile, one-car ownership accounted for around 44% at the beginning of the period. Although this figure fluctuated moderately over the following decades, it remained broadly stable and finished at approximately 42% in 2007.

By comparison, the percentages of households owning two cars or at least three cars both increased throughout the period. The proportion of two-car households rose significantly from about 7% in 1971 to around 26% by 2007.
