The bar chart illustrates the total internet population in Greece, Denmark, and Canada, measured in thousands, from 1998 to 2013, alongside the world average for those years.

Overall, the number of online users in Denmark and Canada considerably outstripped that of the remaining countries. At the opposite end of the spectrum, although Greece witnessed a markedly lower level of internet penetration, with its figures broadly mirroring the world average, both gradually increased just as other countries did.

Looking at the graph more closely, one can see that Denmark had the most internet access, which roughly quadrupled from 23,000 to 95,000 over the 15 years. In 1998, it had the second-highest number of internet users, but it had surpassed Canada by 2003. Whereas growth after 2003 for Canada was slow, the nation's overall number of users ultimately expanded by more than threefold from 1998 to 2013. Additionally, Greece experienced gains of about 20,000 new users every five years. The world average climbed as well, moving from about 3,000 users to 35,000 users, but the gap between it and the three countries included in the chart had widened at the end of the period in question.
