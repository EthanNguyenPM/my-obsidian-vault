The bar chart illustrates the employment rate between men and women across four different countries, namely South Africa, Chile, Finland, and Norway, in 2002.

Overall, male employment rates exceeded those of females in all four nations. Notably, the figures for both sexes in Norway and Finland were relatively similar, whereas South Africa and Chile showed more pronounced disparities, with Chile recording the largest gender gap of all.

Looking graph more closely, Norwegian men had the highest employment rate at approximately 80%, followed closely by Chilean and Finnish men at around 70%. South Africa lagged behind, with only half of its male population in employment.

Regarding female employment, Norway again led with over 70%, while Finland came second at above 61%. In contrast, South Africa and Chile recorded considerably lower figures at 38% and 32% respectively. Notably, Chile's female employment rate was half that of its male counterpart, making it a country with the most significant gender disparity.
