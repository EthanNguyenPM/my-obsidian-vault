The two bar charts compare the numbers of British and American tourists in their respective countries, as well as their expenditure, between 2011 and 2015.

Overall, it is noticeable that the number of UK visitors to the USA outstripped the number of US visitors to the UK throughout the periods. A similar pattern was mirrored in spending, with UK tourists disbursing significantly more money in the US than Americans spent in the UK. Both figures exhibited an upward trajectory toward the end of the period.

Regarding visitor numbers, UK sightseeing goers to the US stood out at approximately 3,200 thousand in 2011, declined slightly to around 2,750 thousand in 2012 and 2013, before recovering steadily to reach a peak of precisely 3,500 thousand in 2015. By contrast, the number of US tourists to the UK remains relatively stable, fluctuating narrowly between roughly 2,600 and 2,800 thousand, and then increased modestly over the final two years, reaching a point of 3,200 thousand in 2015.

Turning to expenditure, British tourists' spending was relatively modest in the first three years, ranging from USD 3,800 million to USD 4,100 million, before surging sharply over the following years to culminate at USD 5,800 million in 2015.
