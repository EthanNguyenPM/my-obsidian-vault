The diagram illustrates how a modern landfill for household waste is constructed, used, and closed.

Overall, the process involves three primary stages, beginning with excavation and preparation of a hole and ending with the sealing of the filled landfill. The structure comprises protective layers and a drainage system, while a separate pipe allows the gases to escape after closure.

Initially, a large man-made hole is dug into natural rock. The sides and bottom of this cavity are lined with clay, followed by a synthetic lining on the inside. Drainage pipes are installed alongside the base, together with a drainage tank beneath the bottom of the hole.

Once construction is complete, household waste is transported by trucks and deposited into the prepared cavity. As the waste accumulates, liquid from it flows down to the drainage system and is collected in the tank below.

In the final stage, the landfill is closed by placing a clay cover over accumulated waste. The waste remains enclosed within the lined cavity, while gases are released through a pipe that extends from the bottom of the landfill to the outside.
