_The diagram illustrates the structure of a smokehouse and explains how it operates to smoke food.

_Overall, the system consists of three connected sections, including an air inlet, a fire chamber, and a smoking chamber housing suspended food. The air travels underground to support the fire, while the resulting smoke flows through a pipe and rises into the food compartment.

_The first section is a vertical shaft extending below ground level, with a lid at the top. Its base is connected with a horizontal passage to a second chamber, which contains logs and a fire. This chamber also has a lid. A pipe leads from its lower ground level to the base of the smoking chamber. Above its base is a box, in which food is hung from the top, with bricks underneath.

_To operate the smokery, the lid over the air inlet is left partly open, allowing air to enter and pass down the shaft. It then travels through the underground passage to the fire chamber, where the logs are burned. The smoke produced is channeled through the connecting pipe to the third section. It rises past the bricks and circulates around the hanging food, smoking it without direct exposure to fire.
