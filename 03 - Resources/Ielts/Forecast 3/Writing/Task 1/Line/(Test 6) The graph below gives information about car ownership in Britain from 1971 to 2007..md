_The line chart compares the percentages of households in the UK owning no car, one car, two cars, or three or more cars, from 1971 to 2007.

_Overall, the proportion of households without a car declined significantly, while ownership of two or more cars became increasingly common. One-car households remained relatively stable and represented the largest category for most of the period.

_In 1971, approximately 48% of households had no car, compared with around 44% owning one. The figure for households with no car fell steadily, reaching roughly 40% in 1979 and 30% in 1995. It continued to drop to around 25% by 2003, before edging up to 26% in 2007. By contrast, the proportion owning one car fluctuated within a narrow range of approximately 41% to 46%, ending at around 42%.

_Meanwhile, the percentage of households owning two cars increased from 7% in 1971 to 10% over the following 4 years. After remaining unchanged between 1995 and 1997, it rose to about 19% in 1991 and continued climbing, reaching nearly 26% by 2007. Households with three or more cars consistently accounted for the smallest share, although their proportion grew gradually from around 1% to 6% over the period.
