_The line chart illustrates the annual number of immigrants arriving in the USA, Canada and Australia between 1990 and 2001.

_Overall, the USA consistently received considerably more immigrants than the other two countries, although its figure declined overall. By contrast, immigration to Canada and Australia increased over the period, with Australia recording the lowest numbers throughout.

_In 1991, approximately 1.45 million people immigrated to the USA. This figure rose sharply to a peak of 1.8 million in 1992 before falling to around 1 million the following year. It then declined gradually to approximately 770,000 in 1996. After a brief recovery to about 930,000 in 1997, the number fell to a low of roughly 690,000 in both 1999 and 2000, before rebounding to around 880,000 in 2001.

_Canada received approximately 200,000 immigrants in 1991, compared with about 110,000 in Australia. The Canadian figure rose to roughly 270,000 in 1993 and 1994, then fell to around 230,000 and remained stable until 1999. It subsequently increased to approximately 270,000 in 2000 and 460,000 in 2001. Meanwhile, Australia's figure dropped to about 70,000 in 1994 and 1995, recovered to around 110,000 in 1996, and stayed at this level through 1999. It then rose to approximately 150,000 in 2000 and remained unchanged in the final year._
