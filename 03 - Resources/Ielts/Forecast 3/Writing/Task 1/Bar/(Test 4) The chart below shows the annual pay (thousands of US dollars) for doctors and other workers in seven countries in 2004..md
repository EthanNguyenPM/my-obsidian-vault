_The bar chart compares the annual pay for doctors and other workers across seven countries in 2004, measured in thousands of US dollars.

_Overall, doctors earned considerably more than other workers in every country, and the US recorded the highest salaries for both groups. Doctors' pay was the lowest in Finland, while Germany, the Czech Republic, and Italy shared the lowest figure for other workers.

_In the US, doctors earned approximately $120,000 annually, compared with about $45,000 for other workers. France and Switzerland posted the next highest doctors' salaries, at around $72,000 and $70,000, respectively. However, other workers earned more in Switzerland than in France, with figures of nearly $40,000 and $30,000.

_Turning to the remaining countries, doctors' annual pay stood at roughly $67,000 in the Czech Republic, followed by those in Germany at about $63,000, and Italy at $60,000. Doctors in Italy earned the least, at around $51,000. Other workers in Finland had an annual salary of nearly $25,000, whereas their counterparts in Germany, the Czech Republic, and Italy each received $20,000. Doctors, therefore, earned about three times as much as other workers in these last countries.
