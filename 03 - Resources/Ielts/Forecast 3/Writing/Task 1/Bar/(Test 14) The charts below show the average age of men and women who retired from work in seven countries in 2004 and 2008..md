_The bar charts compare the average retirement ages of men and women across seven different countries in 2004 and 2008.

_Overall, Sweden recorded the highest average retirement age for both genders in both years, while France registered the lowest. Most figures increased over the period, although the retirement age remained unchanged for Italian men and declined slightly for French and Italian women.

_Among men, the Swedish figure rose from approximately 63.2 to 63.5 years. The Netherlands and the UK also recorded relatively high retirement ages, increasing from 62.6 to 63 and from 62.5 to 62.9 years, respectively. Denmark's figure climbed from 62 to approximately 62.6 years. Germany experienced the largest increase in men's average retirement age, from 61 to just over 62 years, while France saw a smaller rise from 58.7 to 59 years. Italian men retired at an average age of 60 in both years.

_For women, Sweden posted the highest average retirement age in both years, rising from 63 to 63.5 years. The figures for the Netherlands and the UK rose from 62 to around 62.5 and from 61 to about 61.6, respectively. Denmark and Germany also experienced increases, reaching approximately 60.5 and 61 years in 2008. By contrast, the French figure fell slightly from around 58.8 to 58.6, while Italy's declined from 60 to approximately 59.6.
