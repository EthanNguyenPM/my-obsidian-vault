_The bar charts compare the proportions of male and female students in four age groups studying part-time and full-time in Australia in 2006.

_Overall, the highest figures for both modes of study were recorded among 20-24-year-olds, with particularly large percentages studying part-time. Full-time figures varied less across age groups, and full-time study exceeded part-time study for both males and females aged 30 and above.

_Regarding part-time education, the figures for students aged 20-24 were substantially higher than those for other age groups, at 40% for males and 42% for females. Among 15-19-year-olds, the female figure was also higher, at 17%, compared with 12% for males. Both genders recorded 8% in the 25-29 age group. In the oldest category, the male figure stood at 8%, exceeding the female figure of 6%.

_For full-time education, the percentages within the 20-24 age group were 12% for males and 13% for females. The corresponding figures for those aged 30 and above were slightly lower, at 10% and 12%, respectively. Among the youngest category, males recorded 8%, compared with 7% for females. Finally, in the 25-29 age group, the figure of 6% was recorded for males, two percentage points lower than that for females
