_The bar chart compares the proportions of expenditure allocated to five major categories in four countries in 2009, namely the US, Canada, the UK, and Japan.

_Overall, housing accounted for the largest share of expenditure in three countries, while food was the biggest category in Japan. Healthcare and clothing represented considerably smaller proportions than the other three categories across all four nations.

_Housing accounted for approximately 26% of total expenditure in the US, followed by 24% in the UK, 22% in Japan, and 21% in Canada. Japan devoted the largest percentage to food, at around 23%, compared with 20% in the UK and 15% and 14% in Canada and the US, respectively. Transportation represented one-fifth of spending in Canada and 18% in the US, while the corresponding figures for the UK and Japan were lower, at 15% and 10%.

_Turning to the remaining categories, the US recorded the highest proportion spent on healthcare, at about 7%. Canada and Japan each allocated approximately 4% to this category, whereas the figure for the UK was just 1%. The percentages for clothing were more similar across the countries, ranging from around 4% in the US to 6% in Canada, with the UK and Japan at approximately 5.5% and 4.5%, respectively.
