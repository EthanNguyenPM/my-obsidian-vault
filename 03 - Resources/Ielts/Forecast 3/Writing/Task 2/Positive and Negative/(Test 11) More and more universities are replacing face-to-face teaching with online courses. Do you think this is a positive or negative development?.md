_The diffusion of digital technology has facilitated the transition from traditional classroom learning to online courses. Although the flexibility offered by online learning benefits both teachers and learners, I consider that the replacement of face-to-face teaching is largely negative because it can reduce meaningful interaction and limit opportunities for practical training.

_Online education undoubtedly offers flexibility. Students who live far from universities can attend courses without relocating or spending hours commuting, while those with busy work schedules or family responsibilities may find it easier to organize their studies around commitments. Recorded lectures also allow learners to revisit complex material at their own pace. These advantages can create opportunities for people who would otherwise struggle to attend university.

_Nevertheless, replacing traditional classroom learning can reduce the opportunities for immediate discussion and individual support. In a physical classroom, students can ask spontaneous questions, exchange ideas with classmates, and approach lecturers after a lesson. Although online platforms can support similar activities, large remote classes may make it easier for students to remain passive or disengage. Studying alone also requires considerable self-discipline, and some learners may neglect their studies without the routine and accountability of attending classes in person.

_A further concern is that certain facets of university education cannot be adequately reproduced online. Students in subjects such as chemistry or nursing need extensive hands-on experience with equipment and procedures under supervision. Digital demonstrations can prepare them for these tasks, but they cannot fully replace supervised practical training. Campus life also provides informal opportunities to form friendships and develop teamwork skills. A blended approach would preserve these experiences while retaining the convenience of online resources.  

_In conclusion, studying on online platforms offers useful flexibility and broadens access, yet I believe replacing face-to-face teaching is a generally negative development. Universities should draw on online courses to complement in-person instruction rather than eliminate the practical and interpersonal experiences that campuses provide.
