_The contemporary landscape of some children being pressured by their parents has become common nowadays. Although this parental method brings several benefits for children's achievement, I believe this is a negative development, as it compromises their mental health and freedom to pursue their interests.

_There are several reasons explaining why parents exert a lot of pressure on their children. First and foremost, children are innately susceptible to entertainment distractions, such as social media or video games, which often cause them to lose focus on academic responsibilities. If young learners lack self-discipline, their academic performance will suffer. To prevent this, parents feel compelled to impose strict rules and high expectations to ensure that children take their studies seriously. Furthermore, many parents possess a competitive mindset and a strong desire for their children to excel. This drives them to enroll their offspring in a plethora of extracurricular activities, ranging from music instruments and foreign languages to intensive academic courses. These factors combine to create an environment where hyper-parenting becomes the standard method to ensure a child's future success.

_On the deferred side, chronic academic pressure can severely jeopardize a child's psychological well-being. When forced to meet unrealistic family expectations with minimal time for recreation, students are highly likely to suffer from constant anxiety. Over time, these negative feelings accumulate, frequently leading to clinical depression and emotional instability. For instance, recent educational data reveal a sharp increase in psychological crises among high schoolers overwhelmed by dense after-school tutoring schedules. Being deprived of essential relaxation, these students experience a substantial decline in cognitive performance. Ultimately, this intense pursuit of academic perfectionism backfires, turning a supportive upbringing into a major cause of mental burnout.

_Admittedly, **imposing a certain degree of pressure** can serve as a catalyst for a child's development. However, **moderate expectations and reasonable rules** would effectively support their academic growth while protecting their overall well-being.

_In conclusion, while compelling children to meet family expectations may **yield certain benefits for their future prospects**, excessive strictness severely compromises their mental health. Therefore, I believe that this trend is **ultimately negative unless parents maintain realistic and balanced expectations**.

Some parents place considerable pressure on their children to achieve academic success and develop a wide range of skills. This often reflects anxiety about their children’s future and competition with other families. Although reasonable expectations can encourage effort, I believe excessive pressure is a negative development because it can undermine children’s well-being and independence.

One major reason is that parents associate educational achievement with financial security. They may fear that disappointing examination results will limit their children’s access to university and rewarding careers. Concern about distractions such as social media and video games can reinforce this anxiety, leading parents to impose demanding study schedules. Social comparison is another factor. When parents judge their children’s progress against that of classmates or relatives, they may enroll them in numerous additional classes to prevent them from falling behind. What begins as a desire to provide opportunities can therefore become a constant demand to outperform others.

This pressure can have harmful consequences. Children who feel that parental approval depends on their achievements may become anxious about making mistakes. For example, a student attending tutoring sessions every evening may have insufficient time for rest, friendships or recreation. Persistent demands can consequently reduce the enjoyment of learning and contribute to emotional exhaustion. Children may also abandon interests they genuinely enjoy in favour of activities their parents consider more prestigious, limiting their ability to make independent choices.

Admittedly, clear expectations and reasonable rules can help children develop discipline. However, these benefits do not require excessive pressure. Parents can encourage consistent effort, recognise progress and set realistic goals while allowing time for leisure and personal interests.

In conclusion, parental pressure often stems from concerns about future opportunities and comparisons with others. I consider excessive pressure harmful because its potential costs to children’s well-being and autonomy outweigh any gains in achievement.
