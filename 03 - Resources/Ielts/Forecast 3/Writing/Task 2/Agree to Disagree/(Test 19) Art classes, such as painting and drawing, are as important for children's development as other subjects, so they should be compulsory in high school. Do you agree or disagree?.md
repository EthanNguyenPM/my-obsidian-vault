_**Opinions are divided on whether** art education deserves equal priority to other academic subjects and should therefore be mandated in the high school curriculum. **Personally, I side with the view that** artistic training should remain a voluntary elective rather than a compulsory discipline.

_**To begin with,** integrating arts education as a mandatory subject with formal assessment can lead to severe psychological consequences for students. When creative activities like sketching and drawing are tied to strict credit requirements and marking criteria, they inevitably **devolve into an undue academic burden**. Under the constant pressure of examinations, learners have a propensity to abandon genuine creativity, reducing their artistic efforts to **mere compliance and perfunctory work** simply to secure high marks. This institutionalized shift in purpose completely backfires, as it **stifles students' natural affinity** for artistic expression and transforms a relaxing activity into a source of stress.

_**Furthermore,** compelling students to study art **necessitates a reduction in curriculum hours** dedicated to other foundational disciplines. Time spent on arts could otherwise be allocated to core subjects like mathematics and science, or pragmatic skills such as financial literacy and informatics. Consequently, upon graduation, school-leavers may **find themselves ill-equipped with the practical professional competencies** required to compete effectively in the modern labor market.

_**Admittedly,** creative outlets are undeniable catalysts for developing deep observation and cognitive flexibility. **However, I would argue that** these artistic capabilities do not require formal schooling and can be easily practiced at home during one's leisure time. **With the proliferation of digital technology**, students can now effortlessly access online tutorials and guided instructions tailored to their own pace without compromising their school hours.

_**In conclusion,** while arts education is beneficial for personal development, making it compulsory compromises the time allocation for core subjects and **hinders students' readiness for future career pursuits**. Therefore, I firmly believe that art should remain an optional pathway rather than a mandatory requirement.
